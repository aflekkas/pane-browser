## Corrections to the spec

### (FATAL) "Orion browser (Kagi): the closest analogue — a WebKit browser on macOS by a small team."

**Spec said:** "Orion browser (Kagi): the closest analogue — a WebKit browser on macOS by a small team."

**Reality:** Closest PRODUCT analogue, not closest ARCHITECTURAL analogue, and the difference is the whole point. Orion forks WebKit and ports WebExtension APIs into the engine itself — Kagi's own words are "forking WebKit, porting hundreds of APIs, and writing a browser app from scratch." Orion's release numbers track WebKit's (Orion 129 = WebKit 129). Nothing about Orion's extension architecture is reachable from system WKWebView. Six devs, six years, and they are at ~70% API coverage. If Pane's plan borrows anything from "Orion did it with a small team," that inference is wrong — Orion's team is small relative to Chrome, not relative to one person, and they own an engine fork.

**Source:** https://blog.kagi.com/orion

### (MAJOR) Implicitly frames extensions as Orion's unreproducible moat.

**Spec said:** Implicitly frames extensions as Orion's unreproducible moat.

**Reality:** Apple commoditised a large part of it. WKWebExtension / WKWebExtensionContext / WKWebExtensionController are public API since macOS 15.4, and WebKit's own Tools/MiniBrowser/mac ships an ExtensionManagerWindowController demonstrating the integration. Pane targets macOS 26, so this is unconditionally available. **Source:** https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/


### (MAJOR) "Content blocking: WKContentRuleListStore and converting EasyList. Is that viable?"

**Spec said:** "Content blocking: WKContentRuleListStore and converting EasyList. Is that viable?"

**Reality:** Viable but with three hard constraints the framing hides. (1) 150,000 rules per compiled list is a hard cap in WebKit source (maxRuleCount), and the request to raise it to 300k has sat unresolved since 2019 — EasyList+AdGuard base alone is ~100k, so you will be splitting across multiple WKContentRuleLists and adding them all to one WKUserContentController. (2) The rule vocabulary is four actions (block, block-cookies, css-display-none, ignore-previous-rules) and five trigger keys — no scriptlets, no procedural cosmetic filtering, no dynamic per-request decisions. uBlock-grade blocking is not reachable. (3) The obvious converter, AdguardTeam/SafariConverterLib, is GPL-3.0; and EasyList is GPLv3-or-CC-BY-SA-3.0. You must take the CC BY-SA branch for the data and either write your own converter or accept GPL.

**Source:** https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=205719


### (MINOR) "Why did The Browser Company abandon it [Arc]?"

**Spec said:** "Why did The Browser Company abandon it [Arc]?"

**Reality:** Precisely: maintenance mode announced May 2025 for product reasons (novelty tax — "too different, with too many new things to learn, for too little reward"; 5.52% of DAU used multiple Spaces; Calendar Preview at 0.4% adoption), then the company was acquired by Atlassian for $610M in Sept 2025. Two separate events. The product lesson matters more than the corporate one, and it cuts directly against copying Arc wholesale: the mechanics Pane most wants to clone are the ones Arc's own telemetry says almost nobody used.

**Source:** https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/letter-to-arc-members-2025


### (MAJOR) "is it [DDG] the reference implementation to study or even fork?"

**Spec said:** "is it [DDG] the reference implementation to study or even fork?"

**Reality:** Study, never fork. Apache-2.0 is permissive enough legally, but the codebase is welded to DuckDuckGo's backend: remote privacy-configuration JSON, feature-flag service, Tracker Radar CDN, Email Protection, Sync, subscription/VPN entitlements, and DDG-specific signing config. Fork BareBonesBrowserKit (also Apache-2.0, tiny) if you want a permissive starting point; lift TrackerRadarKit (Apache-2.0, 990 KB) wholesale for rule generation.

**Source:** https://github.com/duckduckgo/apple-browsers


### (MINOR) Zen assessed as maybe-worth-the-clone.

**Spec said:** Zen assessed as maybe-worth-the-clone.

**Reality:** Clone it — it costs nothing. The repo is 5.8 MB, not a Firefox checkout, because src/ is a patch overlay applied to mozilla-central by their build tool. ## Recommended approach

Read in this order, and clone only two things.

1. Ora (github.com/the-ora/browser, GPL-3.0) — clone. It is Pane minus a year. Read `ora/Features/Tabs/{Models,State,Browser}` for the tab model, `ora/Features/Sidebar` for the vertical UI, `ora/Features/Privacy` for content blocking, and commit #243 "space-scoped privacy protections" for how Spaces map onto storage. Confirm whether it uses `WKWebsiteDataStore(forIdentifier:)` per space — that's the API you want either way (macOS 14+, stores at ~/Library/WebKit/WebsiteDataStore/<UUID>). Read for design, do not paste: GPL-3.0.

2. Zen (github.com/zen-browser/desktop, MPL-2.0) — clone, 5.8 MB, read `src/zen/` only. Treat `glance/ZenGlanceManager.mjs`, `split-view/ZenViewSplitter.mjs`, `spaces/`, `compact-mode/`, `folders/` as behavioural specs. Glance's real content: hidden child browser in an overlay, parent scaled to 0.97 at 30% opacity, snapshot animated along a computed arc from the click point, parent/child correlated by a `glance-id` UUID. In AppKit that is a child `WKWebView` in an `NSVisualEffectView` overlay plus a Core Animation arc — a day, not a month.

3. DDG (github.com/duckduckgo/apple-browsers, Apache-2.0) — do NOT clone 735 MB. Read four files on the web: `macOS/DuckDuckGo/Tab/Model/Tab.swift` (the TabExtensions composition pattern is the best idea in the repo), plus `SharedPackages/BrowserServicesKit/.../ContentBlocking/{ContentBlockerRulesManager,ContentBlockingRulesCompilationTask,ContentBlockerRulesIdentifier,LastCompiledRulesStore}.swift`. Compilation is slow enough that they built identifier-keyed caching, a last-known-good store, and a failed-compilation store — copy that shape. Then add `duckduckgo/TrackerRadarKit` (Apache-2.0, 990 KB) as an SPM dependency for rule generation and skip EasyList entirely on v1.

4. Extensions: build against `WKWebExtensionController` (macOS 15.4+), using WebKit's `Tools/MiniBrowser/mac/ExtensionManagerWindowController.m` as the reference. Do not attempt Orion's approach.

5. Content blocking: split rules across multiple `WKContentRuleList`s (150k cap each), add all to one `WKUserContentController`. If you later want EasyList, take it under CC BY-SA 3.0 with attribution, not GPLv3, and write your own converter rather than linking GPL-3.0 SafariConverterLib.

Arc: copy Pinned/Favorites and the command bar; treat 12-hour auto-archive and multi-Space as opt-in, since Arc's own numbers (5.52% multi-Space DAU) say the defaults were wrong.


## Effort estimate

Research/absorption: 3-5 days (1 day Ora, 1 day Zen's five manager files, 1 day DDG's content-blocking pipeline + Tab.swift, 1-2 days building a throwaway WKWebExtensionController spike against MiniBrowser to measure real extension coverage before committing to it as a feature). Implementing the copied mechanics on top of an existing tab shell: Spaces-as-profiles via WKWebsiteDataStore(forIdentifier:) ~3-5 days; Arc-style three-tier tab model with auto-archive ~5-8 days; split view ~4-6 days; Glance ~2-4 days; compact mode ~2-3 days; content blocking with TrackerRadarKit + multi-list splitting + compile caching ~5-8 days. Total ~30-45 dev days for the prior-art-derived feature set, assuming a working AppKit+WKWebView shell already exists. Cost is dominated by (a) Spaces/profile isolation, because it touches persistence, session restore, and every WKWebView construction site, and (b) the content-blocking compile pipeline, where DDG's caching machinery exists precisely because naive compilation is unacceptably slow. The single biggest schedule risk is not in this list: it is treating Orion as an achievability proof. It isn't one.


## Open questions

- Does Ora actually use WKWebsiteDataStore(forIdentifier:) per Space, or does it fake isolation? Needs a source read of ora/Features/Privacy and the #243 commit — the DeepWiki index does not say, and this determines whether Spaces-as-profiles is a solved pattern or an open one.
- What is Apple's real WKWebExtension API coverage on macOS 26 versus Orion's ~70%? Nobody has published a comparison. This needs an empirical spike: install uBlock Origin Lite, 1Password, Dark Reader, Bitwarden, Vimium into a MiniBrowser-derived harness and record what breaks. Until measured, 'Pane supports extensions' is unfounded.
- Is Ora abandoned or just paused? Last commit 2026-04-07, four months quiet at v0.2.14. If abandoned, ask why — a solo/small team hitting a wall on a WKWebView browser is exactly the failure mode Pane needs to understand, and the issue tracker (31 open) may name it.
- Does GPL-3.0 on Ora rule out reading it at all for a proprietary Pane? Reading for architectural understanding is normal practice, but if Pane will ever be commercial and closed, consider whether a clean-room boundary is worth maintaining, and get that answered before anyone reads TabManager line by line.
- What does Pane do about Google OAuth returning disallowed_useragent? Firefox iOS and DDG macOS both ship into this wall. Their mitigations are in-repo and worth finding — this affects whether Pane is usable as a daily driver at all, and no prior-art source I found documents a clean solution.
- Is there DRM (FairPlay/EME) in WKWebView on macOS for a third-party app, and under what entitlement? DDG explicitly ships DRM on Android only and has not addressed macOS. Netflix/Spotify working or not is a go/no-go for daily-driver status and is genuinely unresolved from public sources.
- Should Pane use the new SwiftUI WebKit API (WebView/WebPage, macOS 26) or raw WKWebView? Every piece of prior art predates it, so there is zero prior art to copy — meaning either a real advantage or an unpaved road with no escape hatch. Needs its own spike.
- Does the 150k-rule cap apply per list or in aggregate across lists added to one WKUserContentController? The source enforces it per parsed JSON array; aggregate behaviour (memory, matching cost, compile time with N lists) is undocumented and needs measurement before committing to a multi-list strategy.
